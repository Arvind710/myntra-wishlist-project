What raising wishlist-to-purchase conversion sets off.

Two arms carry revenue and one loops back. Buying more of what was saved lifts purchase frequency and the commission on it, and it produces first-party data on what actually gets bought — which is what advertisers pay for. That same data sharpens discovery, tells sellers what to list, and plans stock and delivery better; those improve the experience and put more worth saving back on the list. The dashed arm is what makes this a project rather than a promotion: a discount lifts conversion once and costs margin every time, while a converted save leaves data behind that makes the next one easier.
