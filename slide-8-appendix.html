SUPPORTING ARTEFACT · LINKED FROM SLIDE 8 · THE ENGINE

The research the engine rests on.

Fabric Match assumes two things: that a machine can read a fabric's physical properties from a video and a spec sheet, and that telling a shopper what those properties feel like — against something they already own — changes the decision. These are the papers behind both halves.

01 · Can a model actually read fabric?
01
A Systematic Review of AI-Driven Prediction of Fabric Properties and Handfeel
Tu, Kwan, Yick et al. · Materials (Basel) · 2024
A review of the AI methods used to predict fabric characteristics, covering how the models work, what data they are trained on and how accurate they are. This is the premise under the Engine step — that weight, drape and stretch are machine-predictable properties rather than things only a hand can judge, and that the accuracy of that prediction is already a measured quantity in the literature.
pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11509711/
02 · Does the shopper actually have this problem?
02
Need for touch and haptic imagery: An investigation in online fashion shopping
Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services · 2021
Shoppers hold back from buying a garment online because the sensory experience is missing, and the paper looks for cues that can stand in for touch. This is the case for a reference frame — the gap is not a shortage of specifications, it is the absence of something to feel, and a described comparison against a garment already owned is exactly the compensatory cue this literature is asking for.
sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0969698920313862
03
Increasing fabric perception accuracy of consumers in online fashion retailing
International Journal of Retail & Distribution Management · 2025
How accurately shoppers perceive fabric from an online listing, and what raises that accuracy. This is the outcome Fabric Match is aiming at: not more information on the page, but a closer match between what the shopper expects the fabric to be and what arrives in the parcel.
sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S0959055225000245
What these papers do not establish. None of them tests Fabric Match. They establish that the properties are predictable and that the perception gap is real and movable — the specific claim that a one-sentence comparison against a kept garment closes the decision is this project's own, and is what the user research on slide 5 is for.
