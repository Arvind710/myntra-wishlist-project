Six steps, and the technique inside each one.

The slide gives the plain-English version. This is the same sequence with the method named, so anyone can see what is actually being run and where the judgement calls sit.

STEP 1
Collect
Everything public that touches saving items and buying them, from three kinds of source: long comment threads under hauls and reviews, app-store reviews, and Indian fashion and shopping forums. Reddit's own API access was refused, so it was collected through a third-party service.
scrapers + REST on a schedule
per-query and per-subreddit yield measured before anything is dropped
PII replaced with typed placeholders · Hinglish and Devanagari detected and kept
STEP 2
Filter
Duplicates removed, then a relevance judgement on every surviving record, one at a time. The rule for keeping a record was written down and committed before collection began, so it cannot be loosened later to rescue a result. Every rejection stores its reason.
exact-hash dedupe
MinHash / Jaccard near-duplicate, within one author only — fifty people saying the same thing is the finding, not noise
one gpt-5-mini call per record · 12,002 → 1,018
STEP 3
Label
Two passes over each kept record: first where the shopper stopped, then what stopped them. Every label is pinned to a verbatim quote span in the original text. A second, independent pass groups the same records by meaning without ever seeing the label set — the only view of this material not shaped by the categories, and so the only thing that could catch something nobody thought to look for.
codebook frozen and version-stamped before a record was read
classifier: gpt-5, two passes, 2,094 labels applied
blind pass: embeddings → UMAP → HDBSCAN, 21 clusters
STEP 4
Group
Three questions the labelling has already answered decide which group a shopper falls into: is there still intent to buy, is the purchase waiting on something external, and has the decision been made. No second model call and no guessing at motive — the rules are fixed, so every record lands in exactly one group.
deterministic decision rules, no inference
distinctiveness measured as P(reason | group) ÷ P(reason | everyone)
circular associations identified and excluded from the score
STEP 5
Rank
Each reason scores 0 to 1 on five questions — how often it comes up, how hard people work around it, whether the shopper's intent survives it, whether it can be fixed without a discount, and how well evidenced it is. The fourth is the project's own constraint, built into the score rather than applied afterwards. Weights are exposed as sliders, not hard-coded, so a reader who disagrees can move them and watch the order change.
components min–max scaled, weighted sum, weights 0–2, default 1.00
Monte Carlo: 1,000 seeded draws at ±30% · leave-one-out
co-occurrence lift and PMI above a 30-record floor
STEP 6
Ask
A retrieval-augmented chatbot over the same tables. The question is embedded with the same model used on the corpus, matched against the record index by cosine similarity, and the hits are joined back to their rows before anything is written. The answer cites the records it stands on, and figures are quoted from the stored tables rather than recalculated. It is scoped to one question — anything outside the corpus is refused rather than guessed at.
embed question → cosine k-NN over 1,018 record embeddings
join hits to corpus.db rows → assemble context → gpt-5
no answer without a retrieved record to stand on
01 · What stops it fooling itself

Six checks, and they run whether or not they flatter the answer.

2,094
of 2,094 evidence quotes verified word-for-word against the record they came from. A generated claim whose number does not match its row is rejected outright.
21
groups found by a second, blind pass that never sees the codebook. It is the only view of this material not shaped by the categories — so the only thing that can catch a reason nobody thought to list.
6.6×
the quiet stopping points get their chance. Each would have to be under-reported 6.6×, 14.5× and 25.0× before it overtook the leader, against 3× as the most hidden silence anyone can argue for. The threshold is drawn on the chart, not described beside it.
1,000
re-runs of the ranking with every weight shuffled ±30%. A thousand versions of the reader who disagrees with the weighting — and the order barely moves.
30
records is the floor to be ranked at all, however good a score looks. Below 15 it is not shown as a finding.
59
records re-labelled by hand, blind to what the machine decided. Agreement is published for every reason — including the two where it fails.
02 · Where the agreement holds, and where it doesn't

κ is agreement with a human coder, after chance is removed.

0 is a coin flip, 1 is perfect, and 0.60 is the usual bar for reliable. A person hand-labelled a blind sample of 59 records without seeing what the classifier decided.

ReasonκVerdict
“Which size, when every brand runs different?”0.66clears the bar
“Is this the right price, and is now the right time?”0.64clears the bar
“Will it look good on me, with what I already own?”0.52under the bar
“Will the fabric and colour actually look like the photos?”0.43under the bar
“I need to check with someone before I buy this.”0.10unreliable — a lead, not a finding
The winning reason sits at κ 0.43, under the bar. Its rank survives — it leads on volume, on workaround effort and on surviving intent, none of which depend on that boundary. What does not survive is its edge against neighbouring reasons: where “the fabric looks wrong” ends and “the quality looks cheap” begins is not yet settled. That is the first thing the interviews are built to answer.
One reason is unreliable outright. On “I need to check with someone first”, the human coder read it as app permissions where the codebook means another person's approval. It is reported at κ 0.10 and treated as a research lead, never as a finding.
03 · What it cannot tell you

The limits, stated rather than buried.

Every share is a share of conversation, not a drop-off rate. There is no user-level, funnel or revenue data anywhere in this corpus. It measures what gets talked about, weighted by how hard people work around it — which is why the under-reporting test on slide 3 exists at all.
Forgetting is under-counted by construction. Nobody posts about a wishlist they forgot, and nobody writes a review complaining that a list was hard to scroll. A low count on those steps is not evidence they are small — it is evidence the instrument is deaf to them.
Two validation thresholds fail, and are reported as failing. Agreement with the human coder clears the bar for 2 of the 5 reasons with enough labels to measure it, and the relevance recall figure is an estimate rather than a measurement. Neither is restated as passing anywhere in the app.
About a third of the relevant material is Myntra-specific. The rest is Indian online fashion generally, so anything said about Myntra in particular rests on the smaller pool. Google Play and the Apple App Store also share the same review skew, so they are read as one source rather than two.
Which is why the engine sets the agenda and people settle it. Every finding here was carried into the survey and the interviews on slide 5 to be tested against shoppers directly.
